# How to Write a Late Payment Email (With a Follow-Up Schedule)

Most late invoices come from clients who are disorganized, not dishonest. A clear, calm sequence of emails gets most of them paid without hurting the relationship.

## A simple schedule

| When | Email | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 3 days before due | Friendly heads-up | Helpful |
| 1 day after due | Gentle reminder | Neutral |
| 7 days late | Firm follow-up | Direct |
| 14 days late | Escalation (late fee / pause work) | Formal |
| 30 days late | Final notice | Formal, with a deadline |

## What every reminder needs

1. **Invoice number, amount and original due date** in the first two lines.
2. **One clear action:** "Could you confirm the payment date?" works better than a vague "just checking in."
3. **A payment link** so paying takes one click.
4. **A subject line that can be searched**, e.g. "Invoice #1042 – $1,200 – due Oct 15".

## Example: 1 day overdue
> **Subject:** Invoice #1042 – now due
>
> Hi Sam,
>
> A quick note that invoice #1042 for $1,200 was due yesterday (Oct 15). The payment link is here: [link].
>
> Could you let me know when I can expect payment?
>
> Thanks,
> Alex

## Example: 14 days overdue
> **Subject:** Invoice #1042 – 14 days overdue
>
> Hi Sam,
>
> Invoice #1042 ($1,200) is now 14 days past due. As our agreement says, I'll pause work on new deliverables until it's settled, and a late fee of [X]% applies after [date].
>
> If something is holding it up, reply and we'll sort it out.
>
> Alex

## Mistakes to avoid

- Apologizing for asking to be paid.
- Writing long emotional emails. Short and factual works better.
- Threatening a late fee that isn't in your contract.
- Following up with no date on the calendar for the next step.
