# Pitch Tracking for Freelance Writers: A Simple System That Stops Lost Leads

Most freelance writers lose money not on bad pitches but on pitches they forgot to follow up on. A pitch that sits unanswered for three weeks is usually a pitch nobody remembered to chase.

## Track every pitch in one place

Give each pitch a row with five columns: publication, editor, date sent, status, and next follow-up date. Keep it on paper or in a spreadsheet, but keep it somewhere you look every week.

## Set the follow-up date when you send

Decide the follow-up date at the moment you hit send. Five to seven business days is a common window for a first nudge. Writing the date down at send time removes the guesswork later.

## Use a short follow-up

A good nudge is two or three sentences: reference the original pitch, restate the idea in one line, and ask whether the editor would like to see a draft. Do not apologise for following up. It is part of the job.

## Review pitches weekly

Once a week, scan for anything past its follow-up date and any pitch that has gone quiet after two nudges. Close out the dead ones so your list reflects where your money actually is.
