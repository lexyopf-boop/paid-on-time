# How to follow up on a late invoice without losing the client

Most freelancers wait too long, then send one angry email. Here is a calmer sequence that gets paid faster.

## Set the schedule before you send the invoice

- Put your payment terms on the invoice: due date, late fee (if you charge one), and accepted payment methods.
- Decide now when you will chase: 1 day after due, 7 days after, 14 days after.
- Write each follow-up in advance so you are not rewriting it while annoyed.

## The four-step follow-up

1. **Day 1 after due:** a one-line reminder with the invoice number, amount and a link to pay.
2. **Day 7:** ask whether the invoice reached the right person and whether anything is blocking approval.
3. **Day 14:** state the late fee or pause policy and give a firm date.
4. **Day 30:** stop work on new tasks until the balance clears, and say so in writing.

## Keep a log

A simple log stops you guessing. For each invoice record the issue date, due date, days late, every reminder you sent and the reply. Patterns show up fast: one client who always pays at day 40 needs different terms from one who pays at day 5.

## Rules that save money

- Never start a new project for a client with an invoice more than 30 days overdue.
- Ask for a deposit or milestone payment on new work from clients who have paid late before.
- Keep follow-ups short and factual. Tone matters less than dates and amounts.
